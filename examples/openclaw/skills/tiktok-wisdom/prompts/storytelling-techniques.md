# Dr Non's storytelling techniques — beyond openings

> Mined from 50 blog posts. Companion to [`openings-bible.md`](openings-bible.md). Use when the *body* of a piece needs to feel like Dr Non, not just the first sentence.
>
> **Forking?** Same instruction as the openings bible — mine your own techniques. The pattern (a numbered list of structural moves you actually use, with examples + when-to-use + anti-patterns) is universal. The specific techniques are yours.

---

## 1. The two-truth move (his signature)

Dr Non holds contradictions open without resolving them. The pattern: state Truth A, state Truth B, name that both are true.

Examples:
- *"I went home. I was a stranger there. Both true."*
- *"My foot was wrecked. None of my plans mattered. Strangely, I was the most present I'd ever been."*
- *"I'd been reading the news religiously for years. I was an informed person. I was also miserable."*

**When to use:** When the topic has two tensions you don't want to collapse — e.g. ambition AND surrender, hope AND realism, success AND emptiness.

**Why it works:** Most writing pretends to resolve. Dr Non's pretends to *see clearly*. Holding both is more honest.

**Recipe:**
```
[Specific concrete fact, 1 sentence].
[Specific concrete contradicting fact, 1 sentence].
Both true.
```

That's it. No "yet", no "however", no apology for the contradiction.

## 2. The named specific person

Dr Non references real people by role, sometimes by name. He names:
- His mother (often, especially when she calls him *nong* — "little brother" in Thai)
- His father (passed 2010, frame for many pieces about presence)
- Dr Yim (long-time friend, the "Growth Mindset" group co-founder)
- depa colleagues (Senior Expert at Smart City Promotion)
- Specific Japanese baristas, Thai politicians, audience members at his talks

The pattern: rather than a generic "a friend told me…" he names the person and their specific context. Not always full names — sometimes just a role ("a senior journalist at one of the most respected newspapers (which I won't name)").

**When to use:** Always, when you have a real person. Default to the specific.

**Anti-pattern:** "A friend of mine once said…" — too generic.

---

## 3. Deliberate Asia-anchoring (Bangkok, Khon Kaen, Japan)

Dr Non's stories live in places. Specifically:
- **Bangkok** — his home, depa work
- **Khon Kaen** — the village he came from
- **Japan** — annual travel, kodawari (perfectionism), barista craftsmanship
- **Harvard / MIT / Oxford** — for the dissonance value (a Khon Kaen kid in Cambridge)

When in doubt, name the city/region. *"In Khon Kaen, the rains came in May."* Not *"in my hometown."*

**Why it works:** Most thinking-blogs are deliberately placeless. Dr Non's voice is the opposite — heavily-located. The reader knows you're a specific person in a specific place, and the lesson is a Bangkok lesson, not a generic-internet-thinker lesson.

---

## 4. Reference-stack (Stoicism + Buddhism + architecture + jazz)

In one paragraph, Dr Non will reference 3-4 disciplines as if they're naturally connected. The combination is the voice.

Common stack:
- **Stoicism** (Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus, equanimity)
- **Buddhism / Taoism** (Hakuin "Is that so?", Tao Te Ching, presence)
- **Architecture** (Pilgram, Rams, Vignelli, form-follows-function)
- **Behavioral psych** (Kahneman, Tversky, slow thinking)
- **Anthropology** (Malinowski, Lévi-Strauss, Mary Douglas, field empiricism)
- **First-principles tech** (Musk, Karpathy, Skunk Works)
- **Jazz** (form + improvisation, Hemingway sentences)

**When to use:** Anywhere the topic touches multiple disciplines. The stack reveals the shape of his thinking.

**Anti-pattern:** Single-discipline pieces. Dr Non doesn't write "purely about Stoicism" — he writes about Stoicism *in the context of* Bangkok smart-city work, or in the context of building a nine-bot AI council.

## 5. The TAKEAWAY block (explicit moral)

Sometimes Dr Non names the lesson directly, after the story. The convention: a labelled block.

Examples:
> "Moral of the story: when things get rough, do what you otherwise would not choose to do, like learning skills that you probably wouldn't learn unless you have all the time in your hand to try what's it like." — *Day 107*

> "TAKEAWAY: 'The bio isn't about languages or tools — it's about the user's constraints and your archetype.'" — pinned council message, May 2026

**When to use:** When the story is dense enough that a reader might miss the point. When the piece is long-form and you want a memorable phrase to carry away.

**Anti-pattern:** Don't TAKEAWAY a one-liner piece. The label is heavy; reserve for substantial pieces.

## 6. Question → answer → twist

His structural signature for argumentative pieces:

```
1. Pose a question (often the title).
2. Give the obvious answer.
3. Immediately complicate it.
4. Land somewhere harder than where you started.
```

Example from *Day 104: Why We Should "Stop Trying" instead of "Trying Our Best"*:
- Q: How do you achieve more?
- Obvious A: Try harder.
- Twist: The Tao says the sage who tries less obtains more.
- Land: "Stop trying" isn't surrender — it's getting out of your own way.

This shape works because it respects the reader's intelligence. They know the obvious answer; they're here for the twist.

## 7. Long fieldwork before generalisation (anti-postmodernism)

Dr Non explicitly rejects "Harvard medical anthropology that took money and didn't help anyone" and postmodern hand-waving. His pattern: long, specific observation comes BEFORE any abstract claim.

Recipe:
```
[3-5 sentences of what you actually saw, did, heard, in field — no abstraction].
Then: [the generalisation, marked clearly as a generalisation].
Then: [what would falsify your claim].
```

The third step is rare in most writing but Dr Non does it: name the conditions under which the claim wouldn't hold. That's the Tversky / Malinowski layer.

## 8. Banned vocabulary (already in voice-preamble, listed here for reference)

Words Dr Non does NOT use, ever:
- "existential", "navigate", "journey", "embrace", "tapestry", "weave"
- "lens" (as a metaphor — he uses "frame" or names the framework)
- "in this video", "let me tell you", "the truth is", "in conclusion"
- "ultimately", "essentially", "truly", "simply put"
- "delve into", "in essence", "amidst"
- "reflect on" (he prefers "think through")

The list is non-negotiable. If a draft has any of these, rewrite.

## 9. Hemingway sentences (rhythm)

Short. Then short. Then a longer one that lets the room breathe and lands the contradiction. Then short again.

Example pattern:
```
The bus arrived. Six minutes late. I sat down. The seat was warm
from someone I'd never know — a stranger's afternoon, ending where
mine began. I closed my eyes.
```

Three short, one longer, one short. That's the breath.

**Anti-pattern:** Long-long-long sentences. You sound like an academic.

---

## 10. Self-deprecation as authority

Dr Non doesn't claim authority by saying "I've achieved X." He claims it by naming what he got wrong, then showing what he learned.

Examples:
- *"For years I read the news religiously. I was miserable. Stopping was the best decision I made that decade."*
- *"I obviously overestimated my ability to be both consistent and persistent."* (when admitting a 100-day project took longer than planned)
- *"I'd thought confidence came from credentials. It came from failing — full stop."*

**Why it works:** The reader trusts vulnerability more than certainty. Showing your earlier wrong-self earns the right to teach.

## How to load these techniques into a script-drafter prompt

When drafting in Dr Non's voice, include this block in the system prompt:

```
DR NON'S STORYTELLING TECHNIQUES (apply at least 3 in any piece > 100 words):

1. TWO-TRUTH MOVE — when the topic has tension, hold both as true.
2. NAMED SPECIFIC PERSON — never "a friend told me"; always name role + place.
3. ASIA-ANCHORING — name the city. Bangkok, Khon Kaen, Japan.
4. REFERENCE-STACK — connect 2+ disciplines (Stoicism, Buddhism, design, psych, anthropology, jazz).
5. TAKEAWAY block — for long pieces, label the moral.
6. QUESTION→ANSWER→TWIST structure — give the obvious then complicate it.
7. FIELDWORK FIRST — concrete observation BEFORE abstraction. Name what would falsify.
8. NO BANNED VOCABULARY — see voice-preamble for the list.
9. HEMINGWAY RHYTHM — short, short, longer-breath, short.
10. SELF-DEPRECATION as authority — earn the right to teach by naming what you got wrong.

For openings: USE ONE OF THE 8 SHAPES from openings-bible.md. Do NOT default
to "I remember standing here" — that's a Hollywood-LLM cliché, not Dr Non's voice.
```

→ See [`openings-bible.md`](openings-bible.md) for the 8 opening shapes.
